BATTLEFIELDS & TERRAIN (V3.4): Use the Battlefield Terrain selector in the footer. Choose open ground, a hill, forest, mud, shallow river, rocks or mixed terrain. Terrain can decide the entire battle: elevated defenders have stronger melee and ranged advantages, wooded positions sharply reduce enemy missile effectiveness and weaken mounted shock, and deep mud or river water slows cavalry enough to ruin its charge and discipline. Poor footing weakens braced spear walls and increases exhaustion. Commanders seek ridges and forest positions, defend dry crossing approaches, and send cavalry around hazardous ground; their plans still yield to the General’s manual orders. Solid rocks block movement and low-flying arrows. Cavalry needs clear dry ground to build up a strong charge, and all terrain interacts with existing formations and morale. Switching the terrain resets the current test. The default open plain preserves earlier fairness tests.

HORSE MOVEMENT (V3.1): Mounted troops now accelerate and brake instead of instantly changing speed or direction. A horse turns much wider at full gallop, especially when heavily armored or wounded. A mounted charge requires real forward closing speed, and body-to-body collisions absorb momentum. Cavalry ride through an enemy on a committed attack line, break away when surrounded, and regroup for another charge. Mounted archers try to ride out of close threats to recover their shooting distance. Gait animation and hoof dust show speed; the amber bar is horse health and the smaller turquoise bar is horse stamina. Rider and horse health remain separate. A fallen horse cannot be instantly remounted.

COMMANDER INFLUENCE & MOVING FORMATIONS (V3.6): Open 👑 Commanders to set the battle directive: Adaptive, Hold Defensively, Ordered Advance, Aggressive Assault, or Skirmish & Screen. The commander's own personality still affects decisions, but your goal takes priority until the army is in serious danger. Choose Infantry Formation Doctrine: automatic by equipment, Shield Wall, Spear Wall, Open Line, or Loose. A supported shield wall reduces incoming hits and conserves shield stamina; a planted, supported spear wall prepares its anti-cavalry points faster. Walls advance as two ranks but flex into melee so they can still fight. Every advantage depends on real nearby soldiers and appropriate weapons. The General's manual Attack with a selected Shield Wall or Spear Wall can also move as a line.

SQUADS & COMMANDERS (V3.2): Infantry now maintain lanes while advancing and correct drifting ranks. Foot archers stay behind a friendly infantry screen when one exists. Commanders keep a small infantry reserve behind the line, then commit it when troops are under pressure. When enemy archers expose a wing, cavalry first ride around the flank before attacking; mounted movement still uses real acceleration and turning. Commanders display cohesion and reserve status. Manual General orders always win: a selected Infantry, Archer, Cavalry or Horse Archer group follows your standing orders until you Return to AI. These are battlefield test mechanics, not a campaign system.

MORALE & BATTLE OUTCOMES (V3.3): Use the 🛡 Training mode / ⚔ Real battle switch in the footer. Switching restarts the current test. Training keeps invulnerable friendly troops and recurring enemies; Real Battle allows both armies to die or be incapacitated, prevents reinforcements, and enables casualty morale, fleeing, surrender and commander-led rallies. A battle ends when one army can no longer fight; an automatic report shows fatalities, incapacitated, routed, surrendering and still-broken soldiers, plus each commander's fate. Press Fight another battle to retry with the same rules.

POLEARMS & BRACING: Infantry spears have 65 reach and can pair with shields. Long pikes have 89 reach and occupy both hands. Polearm tips cannot hit reliably inside their minimum range, so crowded soldiers use weaker blunt butt strikes. A spearman or pikeman facing a fast-approaching horse automatically plants his feet and raises the weapon over time. A ready frontal brace can halt a charge and wound or dismount the horse, while flanking, attacking before the spear is ready, or exhausting the soldier can break through. The 🛡 Spear Wall formation is available in Classic. Try the new Spears vs Swords and Pikes vs Cavalry presets.

UNIT ROLES: Infantry fight with swords, axes or other melee weapons and never switch to ranged attacks. Archers always carry their selected bow, crossbow or javelins, prefer firing from a distance, and use their melee sidearm only when an enemy enters close-combat range. If out of ammunition, they keep their ranged weapon ready instead of charging with swords. Choose the mixed unit type for infantry and archers. Cavalry ride faster than infantry, hold wider spacing, charge with momentum, and become less effective in sustained close combat. Choose pure Cavalry, 20 infantry / 20 archers / 10 cavalry, or a grand army with all four classes. Mounted archers ride with a lighter compact horse bow (24 arrows, 710-unit range), shoot while circling, and retreat from close threats. Horse armour is separate from rider armour: leather, mail and plate barding reduce damage but slow the mount. Horses have their own health and stamina; losing a horse dismounts its rider, who continues fighting on foot. Any charging horse deals blunt impact damage even without a lance; lances add a piercing hit. Riders disengage to regroup before charging again.

COMMANDERS: Each side has a separate, mortal commander with a battlefield banner. Tactical, Aggressive and Defensive personalities read the battle, issue orders to Infantry, Archers, Cavalry and Mounted Archers, and protect threatened units. Distant units receive the command later; Leadership reduces communication delay. Use 👑 Commanders to override a class or your whole army. Your General orders apply immediately and persist until you press Return to AI. The enemy army can also be manually controlled.

RANGED COMBAT: V2.4 triples maximum and ideal shooting distances. Better experience and ranged familiarity produce tighter shot groups, and experienced archers lead moving targets. Archers can switch to a new enemy during an existing draw rather than repeatedly cancelling shots when their target disappears. Projectiles still travel physically and can miss. War bows carry 18 arrows (fast), crossbows carry 12 bolts (slow reload, strong piercing), and javelins carry 6 throws (short range, powerful impact). Shots follow lofted trajectories over friendly ranks and stop when they reach the ground instead of sliding along it. Lofted attacks fly over allied soldiers throughout their flight; enemy soldiers, shields and armor can still intercept them. Projectiles have travel time, can miss, and interact with directional shields and armor. Experience and independent ranged-weapon familiarity improve accuracy and timing; set Ranged skill separately from melee Mastery in Test gear. Archers draw their melee sidearm only when enemies enter close-combat range; running out of ammunition does not make them charge with swords. A shield is lowered when aiming a two-handed bow or crossbow.

IMPACT REACTIONS: Heavy hits cause knockback and stagger. A successful active block can create a counterattack opening; a weak shield can suffer a GUARD BREAK. Plate and solid shields can stop a swing and recoil the attacker.

Green and red soldiers wear mixed armor and carry different weapons. One-handed swords can use shields; greatswords and axes occupy both hands. Swing stages: wind-up → active hitbox → recovery. In Classic Training Mode your army is invulnerable; Real Battle Mode makes both armies vulnerable.
